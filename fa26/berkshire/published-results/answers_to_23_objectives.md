# Berkshire: answers to all 23 Objectives

Only the three supplied Berkshire workbooks are used. Norfolk-specific questions and the later final shortlist are outside this notebook.

## Q1. How many cases were referred to the prosecutor each year, and how has that changed over the study period?

**Not answerable.** Referrals cannot be counted. Companion: 32,501 recorded filed case-defendant units; filing volume changed -13.1% from 2017 to 2024.

Limitation: No referral intake table or record of cases screened out before filing. Internal case-defendant unit needs a team docket rule.

## Q2. What is the per-capita case rate (cases per 10,000 residents), so counties of different sizes can be compared?

**Not answerable.** No per-capita rate is calculated.

Limitation: Annual county population estimates are absent. A flat or guessed denominator would not answer the question.

## Q3. What share of cases are felonies vs. misdemeanors, and what are the 5 most common offense categories?

**Partial.** 23.4% of recorded criminal filing cases have a felony as their highest source class; 76.6% have a misdemeanor. Top-five representative codes are tabulated.

Limitation: Broad offense categories and within-class statutory seriousness are unavailable. Civil/Unknown-only cases are excluded from the criminal percentage and shown separately.

## Q4. What percentage of referred cases does the prosecutor decline to charge?

**Not answerable.** Declination rate is not measurable.

Limitation: Neither all referred cases nor pre-filing decline decisions are supplied. Post-filing outcomes cannot reconstruct this denominator.

## Q5. How often is the filed charge different from the arrest/referral charge (upgraded, downgraded, unchanged)?

**Not answerable.** Upgrade, downgrade, and unchanged rates are not calculated.

Limitation: No original arrest/referral-charge field, versioned amendments, or validated severity ordering is present. Same-code matches across two extracts do not prove the charge was never changed.

## Q6. What share of cases are diverted to alternative programs instead of prosecuted?

**Not answerable.** A diversion-program participation rate cannot be established.

Limitation: No program enrollment, eligibility, intake, or completion field. Absence of explicit diversion text is not evidence of zero diversion. CWOF remains separate.

## Q7. Of charged cases, what percentage end in dismissal, plea, trial conviction, or acquittal?

**Partial.** 30,601 observed closed criminal case units can be summarized; 18.8% require outcome review. Overall provisional shares are shown with Unknown retained.

Limitation: No complete eventual-outcome history for all charged cases; the case hierarchy and docket rule require team agreement. Diversion participation is not identified.

## Q8. What is the plea rate, and how often do pleas involve a reduced charge?

**Partial.** 14,483 of 30,601 observed disposed criminal case units (47.3%) have at least one Plea label.

Limitation: Original charges and plea agreements are absent, so reduced-charge frequency is not measurable. Plea includes CWOF and unresolved sentence results.

## Q9. What share of cases go to trial, and what is the conviction rate at trial?

**Partial.** 361 observed disposed criminal case units (1.2%) have an explicit Trial label; 17.5% of those trial results are unresolved. The trial-conviction marker share is reportable for this explicit-label subset.

Limitation: Trial labels may not capture every trial; all-charged cases and unknown-route results prevent the literal full-population estimate.

## Q10. What share of convictions result in incarceration vs. probation vs. fines/other?

**Partial.** 6,288 cases have a source-indicated conviction; sentence type is unresolved for 5.3%. Conditional sentence-type shares are tabulated, including Unknown.

Limitation: Conviction and sentence crosswalks remain provisional. Actual imprisonment/time served is not provided; incomplete conviction classification can select the denominator.

## Q11. What is the median sentence length by offense category?

**Partial.** Total case sentence length by offense category is not identifiable. For the limited explicit-custody term companion, 15.1% of cases lack usable maximum actual-term data.

Limitation: No total concurrent/consecutive case sentence, complete term history, or broad offense-category mapping. Available terms cannot be treated as time served.

## Q12. What share of defendants are detained pretrial, and how often is cash bail set?

**Partial.** Bail records link to 16,500 of 32,312 criminal filing case units; 48.9% have no linked first observed event. Selected status counts are shown.

Limitation: No complete defendant bail/custody history, ability-to-pay indicator, or payment/posting ledger. County-wide detention/cash-bail rates are withheld when coverage fails the 20% rule.

## Q13. What is the median time from filing to disposition, and does it differ by case type?

**Partial.** Median 101 days; IQR 35–258, among 27,825 usable case intervals. 2,776 cases have invalid/missing intervals.

Limitation: Initiation is not independently verified as filing date. No complete case-type field in charge files. Disposed-case selection and older/recent cohort differences prevent an all-filed-case expected duration.

## Q14. Do declination, dismissal, and incarceration rates differ by defendant race/ethnicity?

**Partial.** Reportable dismissal/nolle and recorded-incarceration marker comparisons, with White reference ratios, are tabulated; declination is unavailable.

Limitation: No referral screening, separate ethnicity measure, population exposure, prior record, or complete case facts. Denominators differ by metric; these are not causal or population-adjusted disparities.

## Q15. Do those outcomes differ by sex and age group?

**Partial.** Recorded-sex comparisons and Male reference ratios are available for reportable cells. Age-group results are not measurable.

Limitation: No age/date of birth; declination unavailable; administrative sex categories are not complete gender identity. All Q14 outcome and missingness limits also apply.

## Q16. Do racial disparities remain when you compare within the same offense category and prior-record level?

**Not answerable.** The requested offense-by-prior-record controlled comparison cannot be performed.

Limitation: No validated prior record or uniform lookback, complete offense-category mapping, or full criminal history. Same-source-class comparisons alone would not answer this question.

## Q17. Did any of the above metrics shift noticeably around a policy change, new DA term, or 2020?

**Partial.** Recorded filing case volume changed -23.7% from 2019 to 2020. Time trends are shown without causal attribution.

Limitation: No policy/DA-term timeline or counterfactual. Data alone do not identify why a change occurred.

## Q18. How does this county rank against the state average and the other counties on the team’s core metrics (rates from Q4, Q7, Q10, Q14)?

**Not answerable.** No statewide or inter-county ranking is calculated.

Limitation: Other-county datasets, common denominators, validated crosswalks, and comparable case units are absent. Berkshire declination is itself unmeasurable.

## Q19. How does the number of recorded charges per case relate to the time from offense to disposition?

**Partial.** 28,405 linked cases have usable offense-to-disposition intervals; medians and IQRs by charge burden are tabulated.

Limitation: Selected resolved cases only; older offense dates, court transfer, incomplete charge history, and unknown case facts can affect both burden and delay. No unresolved-case forecast.

## Q20. Do dismissal, conviction, and diversion rates differ across courts when comparing cases of the same offense severity?

**Partial.** Reportable same-source-class dismissal/nolle and conviction-marker comparisons are shown by court; diversion is unavailable.

Limitation: Source class is only broad severity. Multiple-court cases, provisional outcomes, missing prior record and case facts limit comparability. No causal court-effect claim.

## Q21. Which agencies account for the largest share of filed charges, and how does their offense mix differ?

**Answerable.** Pittsfield Police Department accounts for 32.4% of recorded filed charge rows. Agency shares, class mix, and full charge-code mix are tabulated.

Limitation: Describes supplied filing activity, not all referrals or agency crime prevalence. Source class/description mix is available; a broad offense-category crosswalk is not.

## Q22. When both amounts are recorded, how often is bail set lower than, equal to, or higher than the amount requested?

**Answerable.** Among 2,407 paired events: lower 39.6%, equal 48.5%, higher 11.8%. Paired events represent 10.8% of the bail-event extract in the window.

Limitation: The answer is conditional on both amounts being recorded; repeated events can weight some cases more heavily. One-paired-event-per-case sensitivity is supplied.

## Q23. How complete are the race, offense severity, judge, and bail-amount fields, and how does completeness differ across counties?

**Partial.** Berkshire field completeness, years available, case-level unresolved shares, and cash-status conditional amount coverage are tabulated.

Limitation: No other-county records are used, so a cross-county completeness difference cannot be calculated. Explicit Unknown and invalid values need separate audit from blanks.
